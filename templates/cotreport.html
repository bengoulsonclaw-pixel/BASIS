Crowded Markets — positioning at a 3-year extreme

How “crowded” is defined. The COT Index places each market's net position within its own trailing 3-year range (0 = most net-short in three years, 100 = most net-long); a market is flagged crowded long at ≥ {{ hi }} and crowded short at ≤ {{ lo }}. These are positioning extremes — the speculative cohort (managed money in commodities, leveraged funds in financials) heavily one-sided — often read as contrarian, since a crowded long has few new buyers left and a crowded short few new sellers.

Source: U.S. CFTC Commitments of Traders, weekly (Tuesday as-of, published Friday), Futures-and-Options combined. Commodities use the Disaggregated report's Managed Money bucket; financial futures (equity indices, rates, FX) use the Traders-in-Financial-Futures Leveraged Funds bucket (managed money has no analogue there). Net = long − short contracts. The COT Index is the net position's position within its own 3-year min–max range; positioning extremes are frequently read as contrarian but are descriptive, not a recommendation. ICE-Europe (Brent, Gasoil, Robusta), Eurex and other non-US markets are not covered by the CFTC and are omitted.
