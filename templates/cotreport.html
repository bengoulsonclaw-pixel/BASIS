Crowded Markets — positioning at a 3-year extreme

How “crowded” is defined. The COT Index places each market's current net position within its own trailing 3-year range: 0 = the most net-short it has been in three years, 100 = the most net-long. A market is flagged crowded long when its COT Index is ≥ {{ hi }} (net positioning near the top of its 3-year range) and crowded short when ≤ {{ lo }} (near the bottom). These are the positioning extremes — the speculative cohort (managed money in commodities, leveraged funds in financials) heavily one-sided — and are often read as contrarian, since a crowded long has few new buyers left to add and a crowded short few new sellers.

Forward Returns After Positioning Extremes — study

What this shows. Limited to the markets flagged crowded right now (the table above): once positioning in a market became crowded, what did the price tend to do next? For each, we take every past occasion its COT Index entered a crowded long zone (rose to ≥ {{ hi }}) or a crowded short zone (fell to ≤ {{ lo }}), and measure the underlying's average price change over the following 4 and 13 weeks.

Sample window. The study looks back over the price history on file — currently from about {{ study_start }} to {{ study_end }} (~{{ study_years }} years for most markets) — and counts, within that span, how many times each market became crowded and what price did next. The Data (yrs) column gives each market's actual depth and n is its number of crowded episodes over that window; read any market showing only a year or two with caution.

How to read each column.  •  n (Long n / Short n) — the sample size: how many separate times the market entered that zone (a multi-week crowded run counts once). A low n means treat the figure with caution.  •  L / S · +4w, +13w — the average price move 4 and 13 weeks after those entries (green = price rose, red = price fell).  •  Base · +4w, +13w — the market's average move over the same horizon across all weeks. The gap between each side and Base is what the extreme has historically added. A descriptive back-look, not a forecast.
