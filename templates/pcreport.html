Options put/call ratios across {{ n_markets }} markets — open interest (standing positioning, the headline) with traded volume (today's flow) alongside  ·  {{ n_put }} put-heavy  ·  {{ n_call }} call-heavy. How it's calculated: the ratio is puts ÷ calls — put open interest ÷ call open interest on the open-interest basis (the headline), and put volume ÷ call volume on the volume basis. A ratio above 1 means more puts than calls (put-heavy / defensive); below 1 means more calls (call-heavy / bullish). Because the “normal” level differs by product (index options sit structurally put-heavy from hedging), each market's OI ratio is scored as a 0–100 percentile within its own trailing-year range — flagged ≥ {{ hi }} (put-heavy) or ≤ {{ lo }} (call-heavy).

Products of Interest — put/call extremes, shifts & divergences

How to read this. Every row is a market currently at a put/call extreme. OI P/C is the put/call ratio on open interest (positions held overnight); Vol P/C is the same on today's volume (what actually traded). Above 1.0 means more puts than calls, below 1.0 more calls. OI %ile ranks today's OI ratio against its own past year: 100 = the most put-heavy (defensive) it has been all year, 0 = the most call-heavy (bullish) — we flag it put-heavy at ≥ {{ hi }} and call-heavy at ≤ {{ lo }}. Δ1d flags positioning that moved sharply today — how big today's one-day change in the ratio is next to its normal daily swing, badged when the jump is unusually large (|z| ≥ {{ spike_z }}). Flow−OI flags when today's trading is running ahead of standing positioning: the volume ranking minus the OI ranking, badged when the gap is ≥ {{ diverge }} points — the day's flow leaning far more one way than the open book. As a rule of thumb, heavy put demand is defensive and heavy call demand bullish — though extremes are frequently read as a contrarian signal.

Source: exchange options open interest and volume, aggregated to puts vs calls per underlying. The put/call ratio is puts ÷ calls; the OI P/C percentile is today's ratio's position within its own trailing 1-year range. A put/call ratio is a sentiment / positioning gauge — heavy put demand is defensive and heavy call demand bullish — and extremes are frequently read as contrarian, but are descriptive, not a recommendation. Coverage depends on each contract's listed-option liquidity; markets without a clean put/call (including some FX, whose vol trades OTC) are omitted.
